# Session D · Aerobic efficiency and environment

4 November 2026 · Research branch verified at `9a02cf8`; PR #34 open, draft and unmerged.

**Question.** When does a change in an ordinary runner's pace or heart rate exceed the variation expected from the runner, the route, the weather and the instruments? Even when it does, what action does the evidence support?

**Decision.** Prioritize speed at comparable easy HR on repeated matched runs. Use HR at fixed speed as a complementary, separately measured endpoint. Show one eligible observation descriptively. Reserve a longitudinal interpretation for replicated changes with an uncertainty interval. A runner or coach can review conflicting effort, execution and context. No candidate here has demonstrated sufficient individual decision utility to unlock progression, reduce training automatically or reset zones.

## Frameworks and conflicting evidence

| Component | Evidence and competing interpretation | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Maffetone 1. Low-intensity aerobic development | Seiler describes predominantly low-intensity work in successful endurance programs. The existing 16-week randomized comparison of 20 recreational marathoners found similar finishing performances for two low-intensity-heavy distributions. It did not isolate an easy-only intervention or establish equivalence. [Seiler](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20861519/) · [trial audit](2026-11-04-polarized-pyramidal-marathon-rct.md) | PARTIAL / MODERATE for including easy work. Neither a personal HR ceiling nor an exclusive training distribution follows. Record three-zone boundaries and whether distribution counts time or session goals. |
| 2. Repeated pace at comparable HR | Standardization makes repeat assessment plausible. The running monitoring review describes variable sensitivity across protocols. A lower HR after intensified work can coexist with fatigue. [Running review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9940993/) · [Haaf](https://doi.org/10.1080/17461391.2019.1571112) | PARTIAL / MODERATE as a field trend candidate. The target can be a familiar, individually observed easy effort. |
| 3. MAF test | The originator describes a warm-up of about 15 minutes and 3–5 track miles with individual splits, consistent conditions and periodic retesting. Published examples illustrate feasibility. No independent validation of the branded monthly change as a health or performance trigger was identified here. [Protocol](https://philmaffetone.com/maf-180-personalizing-exercise-heart-rate/) · [monthly claim](https://philmaffetone.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/MAF-OR-Marathan-Pace-v1.1.pdf) | PARTIAL / LOW for the specific test. Preserve splits, target source, elapsed duration and late fade. A single first-mile best is vulnerable to selection. |
| 4. 180 Formula | Høeg and Maffetone report that 170/223 experienced runners improved 5-km time after gait-derived HR prescription. The formula was derived afterward. The abstract has inconsistent group-2 denominators across methods and results. There is no independent out-of-sample criterion agreement in that result. In 165 recreational runners (64 women), even fixed anchors calibrated from treadmill testing had several bpm of individual LT1 error. That study did not directly test MAF. [Derivation abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.wem.2015.03.015) · [independent anchor study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40088270/) | NOT_APPLICABLE / INSUFFICIENT for personal HR prescription. Reproducible arithmetic supplies no estimate of individual physiological error. |
| 5. Health/status adjustments | The [author's guide](https://philmaffetone.com/resources/ebooks/The-MAF-Method%28v1.0%29.pdf) modifies the age estimate for health and training history. No isolated validation of adjustment size, direction or category reproducibility was identified. Status also changes the ability to complete a test. | NOT_APPLICABLE / INSUFFICIENT for numeric adjustments. Keep voluntarily reported status as context. Do not interpret illness as a request for another test. |
| San Millán and Brooks | Graded exercise with blood lactate and indirect calorimetry compared professional endurance athletes with less-fit groups. Their associations concern substrate use under a measured workload. Brooks's lactate-shuttle interpretation allows simultaneous production, transport and use. A blood concentration alone does not identify flux. Cross-sectional group differences cannot show which HR trains an individual's fat oxidation best. [Joint study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28623613/) | INDIRECT / MODERATE for mechanism. Consumer pace and HR do not measure fat oxidation, mitochondrial function, or either lactate threshold. |
| Jones, Maunder and colleagues | Jones distinguishes fresh capacity from resistance to deterioration. In 82,303 recreational marathon records, internal/external decoupling was associated with performance. Self-selected pacing can influence both. In 31 recreational runners (15 women), LT1 speed fell after 90 minutes at 90% of fresh LT1 speed. HR rose, but HR change did not significantly track individual LT1 loss. A separate well-trained running test–retest study found second-half HR/economy drift only moderately to well repeatable, depending on window. [Jones review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37606604/) · [race cohort](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35511416/) · [Nuuttila, methods and results](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00421-024-05631-y) · [retest study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42050344/) | DIRECT / MODERATE for keeping late-session behavior separate. Agreement with a specific laboratory durability endpoint must be demonstrated. Race association, test repeatability and training benefit are three separate questions. |

## Candidate measurement register

Grades apply to the stated use only. DIRECT means a close context match, PARTIAL requires calibration, INDIRECT supports interpretation, and NOT_APPLICABLE excludes a proposed use. Confidence runs HIGH, MODERATE, LOW, INSUFFICIENT. Each row inherits the environmental, sensor and longitudinal rules below. One observation means one eligible session, regardless of the number of sensor samples. A **block** means at least three matched days as an exploratory feasibility floor. No minimum for individual prescription or progression has been established.

A repeated field observation can motivate asking about fatigue, fueling or conditions. A training decision needs independent corroboration such as effort at the same workload, completion of comparable sessions and the athlete's report. A progression gate would additionally need prospective comparison with the existing policy on outcomes, burden and incorrect decisions. No metric meets that gate.

## Environment and provenance

| What to record | How to compare, and when to abstain |
|---|---|
| Air temperature, relative humidity, paired timestamps and weather source | Evaporative opportunity changes with ambient moisture. Derive dew point or actual vapor pressure from concurrent temperature/RH when useful, retain units and derivation. Treat them as alternative descriptions of moisture. Comparing RH alone across temperatures can mislead. In 11 trained men exercising at 31°C, higher RH impaired running in controlled conditions. [Humidity experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28349085/) |
| Sun or shade, radiant exposure, wind speed and direction relative to travel, acclimation | Direct sun and headwind can vary within a loop. Wind changes both cooling and running demand. Race-level associations across 1,258 events do not estimate a personal easy-pace adjustment. Record station distance, timing and shelter. Mark exposure unknown where street and station differ. [Race analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34652333/) · [heat balance review](https://doi.org/10.1152/physrev.00038.2020) |
| Route, direction, surface, ordered grade profile, turns and stops | Segment a hilly approach from a later flat loop. Match the same direction and same position in the workout. Equal total ascent can mask opposing early/late slopes. Small treadmill studies establish nonlinear grade cost. Downhill pace cannot characterize eccentric load. [Minetti](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12183501/) |
| Absolute altitude and recent altitude exposure separately from cumulative elevation gain | A flat high-altitude run has zero climbing yet different oxygen availability. Record starting/mean altitude and change of residence or travel. Smooth noisy elevation against measured route or barometric reference. Avoid pooling acclimatized and newly arrived sessions. |
| Fuels, fluids, recent effort and recovery | Record meal timing, during-run carbohydrate and fluid, duration, recent long/hard or strength work, sleep, soreness, illness and RPE when available. Missing intake is unknown. Glycogen availability and heat strain can alter late performance together. A cycling intervention supports carbohydrate effects after prolonged work, with uncertain transfer to short easy running. [Carbohydrate trial](https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.70326) |
| Distance, time and HR provenance | Keep source file, device/sensor model, firmware, recording interval, timer vs elapsed vs moving time, lap boundaries and pause flags. Use measured laps and elapsed active segment time for primary speed. GNSS error varies across track, urban and forest settings. Prefer electrical chest HR as study reference, with contact/dropout QC. Wrist optical HR may lag, lock to cadence or disagree by modality. Paired device days bridge a change. An unbridged change starts a separate series. [Eight-watch GNSS validation](https://doi.org/10.2196/17118) · [ECG comparison](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28709155/) |

## Real-data experiment and decision criteria

1. **Admit data first.** Session F verifies consent, permitted use and original device provenance. Keep identifiable tracks outside Git. Recruit across experience levels, sexes, devices and hot/humid routes. One runner can establish personal feasibility. A group rule needs independent runners, dates and adequate subgroup precision.
2. **Freeze windows before outcomes.** Reuse Batch 8F's comparable easy segments and the agenda's initial 20-minute steady segment after warm-up. Match HR within 3 bpm, temperature within 3°C and RH within 10 percentage points only as pilot eligibility settings. Specify fixed-speed stage separately. Match grade, direction, effort and timing. Count rejected sessions and athletes who cannot supply matches. No filling in missing HR or extrapolating beyond common exposures.
3. **Measure noise before change.** Collect repeated ordinary runs on separate days during a stable baseline, then a follow-up block at the same point in the weekly cycle. Three eligible runs per block permit exploration. There is no established number of calendar weeks for a decision. Log days spanned, spacing and autocorrelation. Compare short and longer prespecified blocks, and stop extending a block solely to obtain a favorable result. If uncertainty remains wide, return insufficient evidence.
4. **Estimate individual typical error.** For independent stable duplicate pairs of the same measure, TE = SD(pair differences)/√2 in original units. Inspect systematic bias, proportional error, outliers and sensor effects first. Use log speed for relative error when justified. For independent observations of similar variance, noise SE of a change of block means is TE × √(1/n₁ + 1/n₂). Include uncertainty in TE itself. For serially dependent runs use clustered/block intervals or a repeated-measure model. Never count seconds, laps or overlapping windows as independent days. Separate between-athlete ICC from within-athlete absolute agreement. Size the next cohort using pilot variance and a prespecified practically meaningful change, with allowance for matching losses.
5. **Attack false improvements.** Test sham baseline-to-baseline splits, sensor swaps, reversed directions, warmer follow-up blocks, a hilly first half, ordinary post-strength fatigue and missing RPE/weather. Repeat after removing the best day. Report bias, error intervals, match coverage, missingness and false-alert rates by athlete, device and climate. A between-runner correlation or a narrower error on training data cannot pass this test.
6. **Independent outcome and policy test.** Hold out later dates, then entire athletes. Compare against same-route raw speed and simple RPE/workload baselines. Freeze a meaningful change and tolerable false-alert rate before evaluation. Failure to beat the simple baseline, subgroup sign reversal, poor interval coverage or informative exclusions defeats promotion. Only after repeatability and prediction are shown should a prospective, review-only policy be compared with usual planning. Measure inappropriate progression, unnecessary reductions, athlete overrides and training outcomes separately.
