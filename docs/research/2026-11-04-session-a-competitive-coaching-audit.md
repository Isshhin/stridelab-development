## Seven systems, seven different decisions

The two tables form a single product matrix. Unknown means the reviewed sources did not establish that particular adaptation. In particular, an app reporting a metric does not establish that the metric changed tomorrow's prescription.

## StrideLab capability matrix

Grades compare demonstrated functional scope with the named comparator. Significantly behind means a documented decision loop lacks an equivalent demonstration. Slightly behind means a related contract exists but a narrower step is missing. Potential advantage identifies a testable distinction only. Insufficient evidence means a head-to-head conclusion is unavailable. No row earned near parity from synthetic passes alone.

| Capability | Status | Exact boundary and comparator |
|---|---|---|
| Athlete-state estimation | BEHIND_SIGNIFICANTLY | Garmin consumes ongoing performance/health data. StrideLab has input contracts and provenance work but lacks a field-calibrated within-athlete state estimate. |
| Training-response evaluation | INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE | Runna Score measures plan-relative execution. Neither matched-HR repeatability in StrideLab nor equivalent vendor outcome accuracy is established. |
| Goal-based generation and progression | BEHIND_SIGNIFICANTLY | Runna separates mileage, hard dose and pace across an event plan. No current matched draft comparison demonstrates equivalent StrideLab progression across 5/6/7 days. |
| Weekly volume and session spikes | BEHIND_SLIGHTLY | Baseline/candidate counterfactuals exist. No admitted replay has established when to alter one session while preserving the week, or vice versa. Compare Runna and Kaizen. |
| Intensity and workout-dose adaptation | BEHIND_SIGNIFICANTLY | Runna can change reps independently of target pace. StrideLab has workout revision contracts but lacks athlete-tested attribution of a missed target to pace, rep count or conditions. |
| Workout selection/substitution | BEHIND_SIGNIFICANTLY | Runna changes long-run structure in heat. Fitbod filters substitutions by equipment. No demonstrated equivalent context-sensitive menu with retained goal and dose. |
| Readiness/fatigue | BEHIND_SIGNIFICANTLY | Garmin and TrainerRoad incorporate recovery in subsequent choices. No validated StrideLab reason to change a workout from readiness beyond simpler reports. |
| Environmental prescription | BEHIND_SIGNIFICANTLY | Runna exposes concrete heat choices. StrideLab has a context-matching protocol without a verified recommendation loop. Relative environmental measurement accuracy remains unknown. |
| Missed sessions, illness and re-entry | BEHIND_SLIGHTLY | StrideLab replays interruption states. Runna exposes cause-specific choices. Field tolerance and return dose after comparable absences remain unverified. |
| Load and injury safeguards | INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE | Rules and synthetic invariants can bound exposure. Neither StrideLab nor the compared vendor features establish injury-prevention effectiveness. |
| Longitudinal personalization | BEHIND_SIGNIFICANTLY | Kaizen revises weekly guidance and TrainerRoad revises future workouts. StrideLab can replay event histories but has no prospective evidence of calibrated individual dose selection. |
| Strength adaptation | BEHIND_SIGNIFICANTLY | Fitbod adjusts exercise dose from logs and RP redistributes weekly sets. StrideLab lacks a demonstrated feedback-to-next-lift or next-week allocation comparison. |
| Cross-discipline interference | POTENTIAL_ADVANTAGE | Explicit protected sessions and discipline-native dose could explain competing demands more clearly than DSW's indirect recovery path. Fitbod already considers imported cardio. No demonstrated superiority. |
| Explainability and user control | POTENTIAL_ADVANTAGE | A reviewable proposal with evidence, affected sessions and rollback could outperform automatic Run Coach revision on reconstructability. Runna already offers choices. No usability comparison. |
| Real-data validation | INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE | Published examples and vendor training-log counts have no matched control. The earlier cohort/pilot work does not compare prospective athlete outcomes with any named system. |

## Ranked research decisions

Rank is order of investigation, weighting coaching value and feasibility for the easy-running/threshold use case. H/M/L are ordinal. Evidence rates support for investigating the gap, not evidence the intervention works. Engineering and validation rate effort separately. Applicability refers to transfer into StrideLab, confidence to the candidate proposition. All candidate rules are HYPOTHESIS. These are proposed comparisons within the existing agenda, not executed experiments.

| Rank and exact gap | Value / evidence / engineering / validation | Supporting evidence and contrary finding | Applicability / confidence | Data, experiment and falsifier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Separate hot-day prescription from evidence of fitness change | H/H/M/M | Runna separates Heat and Score. Prior matched-HR review identifies heat, grade and sensor confounding. Strict matching can discard most runs and introduce selection bias. | DIRECT / MODERATE | E009/E010: consented repeated easy segments, grade, sensor, time, humidity, heat, HR and RPE. Freeze matching before analysis. Compare typical error and eligible coverage against unrestricted same-athlete averages. Reject if out-of-sample error does not improve or prespecified coverage fails. Record original and adjusted target separately in a permissioned vendor capture. |
| 2. Distinguish a scheduling miss from illness and unknown history before offering return dose | H/M/M/H | Runna and TrainerRoad accept time-away context. Their rules differ. No validated dose or evidence that a longer pause alone indicates poor readiness. | DIRECT / MODERATE | E012: naturally occurring breaks, reason, actual pre/post dose, symptoms, availability and independent review. Shadow reason-aware vs duration-only proposals for matched gaps. Reject if more hidden catch-up, inappropriate increases or no improvement in blinded appropriateness at comparable deferral. |
| 3. Separate weekly km, longest session and hard-work minutes when frequency changes | H/H/M/M | Runna exposes distinct volume and difficulty controls. Session-spike cohort association differs from weekly exposure. A novice graded-program trial detected no injury reduction. | DIRECT / MODERATE | Complete E007 matched drafts and session reconciliation. Replay consented weeks at equal km with different longest runs and hard minutes against a weekly-only baseline. Reject the extra rule if it flags identical prescriptions indiscriminately or increases unsupported edits without improving blinded review. Vendor agreement is descriptive only. |
| 4. Explain a substitution and let the athlete review affected sessions | H/M/L/M | Garmin Run Coach lacks individual rescheduling. Runna and TrainerRoad offer alternatives, so review alone is not distinctive. Extra prompts may reduce adherence. | DIRECT / MODERATE | E008 source-labeled pairs plus permissioned task trials. Counterbalance automatic vs reviewable proposals on identical calendar conflicts. Record decision reconstruction, task time, mistaken acceptance and lock violations. Reject if reconstruction fails to improve or time/errors outweigh benefit. |
| 5. Keep local lifting dose and adjacent run constraints separate | M/M/M/H | Fitbod considers cardio exposure, RP allocates group sets across lifts. RIR error varies with load and proximity to failure. Concurrent-training results are context dependent. | PARTIAL / MODERATE | Reuse E004/E005 designs with exercise IDs, set/load/RIR, equipment, lower-body session timing, run intent and actual tolerance. Compare split-only scheduling with native-dose proposals, blinded to policy. Reject if substitutions break equipment/volume constraints or leg-day conflicts and unnecessary edits do not improve. |
| 6. Add readiness only when it improves next-session decisions over simple self-report | M/M/M/H | Garmin and TrainerRoad operationalize recovery. A meta-analysis found small, nonsignificant performance and VO₂max differences for HRV-guided vs predefined training. Feature uptake cannot resolve this. | PARTIAL / LOW | Consented within-athlete sleep/HRV coverage, perceived fatigue, recent load and next-session completion/RPE. Athlete-held-out ablation with/without readiness; compare calibration, false downgrades and deferrals. Reject if incremental performance disappears after missingness adjustment or simple fatigue report matches it. |
| 7. Forecast difficulty only after a transparent baseline is calibrated | M/L/H/H | TrainerRoad has a cycling difficulty forecast, Kaizen describes best-case race estimates. Neither validates an equivalent running forecast. | INDIRECT / LOW | Later consenting athletes' planned and actual dose, RPE, partial completion, modifications and phase. Compare frozen history-based baseline with candidate on chronologically later unseen athletes. Reject if ordinal prediction error or calibration does not improve, including interrupted histories. Defer until enough outcomes exist. |

Scientific counterweights: [HRV-guided training meta-analysis](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1440244021001080), [Buist graded-program trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17940147/), [Frandsen running exposure cohort](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2024-109380), [RIR accuracy review](https://doi.org/10.1080/10833196.2025.2564026). The cohort association cannot identify an effective intervention. Estimated reps in reserve and modeled muscle recovery are different measurements. No direct controlled comparison of these seven current products was identified in this targeted review.
