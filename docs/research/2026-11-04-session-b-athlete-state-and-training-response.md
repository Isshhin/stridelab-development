## Question and current gap

What can we say about this runner, for this workout, with the observations available **before** the decision? The [parallel program](2026-11-04-engine2x-parallel-research-program.md) defines the evidence grades. The [focused agenda](2026-11-04-focused-engine2-evidence-and-experiment-agenda.md) already specifies admission, comparable-HR repeatability and environmental exclusions. Existing experiment 005 addresses adaptation-state/load contracts. A contract can establish that a record is handled consistently. The scientific gap is whether an estimate distinguishes two runners, or two days for one runner, with meaningfully different outcomes. No StrideLab result reviewed here establishes that ability.

## Representation and baseline contract · HYPOTHESIS

Store a time-stamped profile. Each estimate carries its own **construct, discipline, horizon, direction or range, last eligible observation, evidence for and against, reference protocol, coverage, quality flags and confidence reason**. Use measured units when available. For latent dimensions allow rising, stable, falling, mixed and unknown. Stable requires repeatable evidence. Recovery is the observed course after a named exposure, not a timer or the inverse of fitness. An athlete can show improving longer-term fitness and elevated short-term fatigue at the same time.

Maintain separate observations, exposure records, interpretations and proposed decisions. Use observation time and time available to the engine. Keep raw/classified/derived lineage so RHR, HRV, sleep and a score from one device do not become independent votes. Record protocol, device, firmware, units, posture, time of day and overnight versus waking method. Compare within athlete and protocol using prior comparable observations only. Plot level and variation, estimate typical error from repeats, display gaps and detect protocol changes. Compare fixed reference blocks with rolling references in shadow testing: a rolling baseline can normalize sustained deterioration. Start a separate series after an incompatible device or protocol change unless overlapping measures establish a bridge. No universal baseline length, decay constant or clinical threshold is established.

Distinguish the literature confidence below from **confidence in today's estimate**. The latter can be unknown even for a well-established measurement: insufficient comparable history, stale observation, a changed device, correlated sources or disagreement each has a different remedy. Abstention is a valid output. Keep observed training history available even when a physiological estimate is withheld.

## Worked interpretations

* **Better easy pace, worse quality tolerance.** Matched easy runs improve beyond observed error. The last two comparable threshold sessions required longer recovery at higher reported effort. Update the easy trend and record quality tolerance as uncertain or concerning. The threshold sessions do not erase the easy trend. Neither observation authorizes faster prescribed threshold pace.
* **Hot, hilly evening after a strength session.** Slower pace, elevated HR and sore quads support several explanations. Preserve environmental exposure and soreness. If no comparable route/conditions exist, the run contributes to performed dose but does not update aerobic efficiency. One weather-confounded run cannot establish detraining.
* **No file on Tuesday.** A confirmed schedule move links the same planned session to its new date. A sync failure leaves dose unknown. A partial run due to time pressure contributes its actual work with no fatigue inference. A symptom-limited stop retains its reason for illness/re-entry review. The same empty calendar cell therefore leads to different records.

## Prospective test and decision boundary

1. **Admission and measurement.** Session F admits only purpose-authorized longitudinal data. Record consent scope, sensor history, plan snapshots, decision times, voluntary responses and missed attempts. Keep raw records outside Git. Check matched-run and threshold repeatability before fitting state trajectories. For an initial single runner report feasibility and personal uncertainty only.
2. **Freeze targets before fitting.** Predict, separately, next comparable quality-session execution at intended effort, late-versus-early response in the next comparable long run and change in an independently measured benchmark over a training block. Define acceptable execution and meaningful benchmark change from protocol and repeatability before opening the evaluation split. Report non-attempts, reason, modified sessions and absence of a comparable endpoint as distinct outcomes. No counterfactual label for an unattempted workout.
3. **Comparators.** Start with planned difficulty, recent actual dose and last comparable result. Add a brief pre-session fatigue/soreness check-in. Then add one family at a time: autonomic, sleep, context, tolerance history, composite score alone. Distinguish within-athlete deviations from between-athlete averages. Compare a personalized simple baseline with a partially pooled model; neither inherits the other's evidence.
4. **Prevent leakage and selection.** Rolling-origin evaluation within athlete, then held-out athletes/devices/seasons where feasible. Fit baselines and select models only on past training folds. Keep all readings from one run together. Blind endpoint review to scores and record whether athlete/coach saw the watch score. Completed-only analysis is secondary. Characterize who skips, changes or omits logging, and stress-test plausible outcomes for missing attempts without labeling them facts.
5. **Judge usefulness.** Report per-athlete error in native units, interval coverage, calibration of categorical predictions, sensitivity to missed poor-tolerance days, false restriction of successful days and abstention coverage. Use athlete-level or within-athlete block uncertainty as appropriate. Report outcomes by training level, sex where available, device and hot/hilly versus matched conditions when sample sizes permit. Set acceptable error/tradeoffs and sample requirements from repeatability, precision and athlete priorities before evaluation. If evidence is sparse, return inconclusive.
6. **Separate prediction from coaching benefit.** If held-out estimates do not beat the simple baseline at comparable coverage, retain descriptive observations only. Even a better forecast warrants shadow evaluation first. A later consented comparison of reviewable decisions against usual planning must test training outcomes and burden. No synthetic pass or predictive association answers whether changing a workout helps.

## Handoff and open questions

**C/E:** Consume observed dose, reason for interruption, restrictions, uncertainty and workout-specific tolerance. Do not infer loss of fitness from attendance or prescribe from a composite. **D:** Own repeatability, environmental comparability and method-specific threshold anchors. **F:** Prioritize planned-to-performed linkage, as-of timestamps, reason for non-attempt and device overlap ahead of more activity-only files. **H:** Contrast Vesterinen's intervention with pooled nulls; keep Foster's internal dose separate from prediction. **A/G:** Garmin's published input list provides a comparator. Gneta adds a direct but limited within-athlete null; it strengthens the need for independent endpoints, without establishing a vendor parity judgment.

Two adjacent methods materially change the validation plan: Nuuttila's classification exposes circular reference labels, and the commercial field analysis exposes selection into completed runs. Both are reasons to revise validation design before adding signals. Open questions include how many comparable repeats each protocol needs, whether check-ins alone perform as well as sensors, which athletes benefit from personal baselines, and how much abstention users can tolerate. Session B is ready for synthesis review. Real-athlete calibration and policy efficacy remain open.
