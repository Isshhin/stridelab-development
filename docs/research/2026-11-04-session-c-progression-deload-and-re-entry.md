## Question and current StrideLab behavior
When should the next planned run or training block change after successful training, accumulating fatigue, a race or an interruption? An ordinary build, a post-race week and a return from illness can all have identical weekly totals and require different decisions.

[Batch 3](batch-3-kaizen-load-and-interruption-adaptation-2026-11-03.md) distinguishes isolated misses and sustained interruption. [Batch 7](batch-7-engine-2-0-architecture-evidence-traceability-and-validation-gates-2026-11-03.md) provides research actions for keeping, revising or deferring plans. [Batch 8F](batch-8f-original-lab-admission-and-field-outcome-protocol-2026-11-03.md) separates synthetic conformance from field outcomes. The [focused agenda](2026-11-04-focused-engine2-evidence-and-experiment-agenda.md) already specifies E011 exposure comparisons and E012 natural interruptions. These documents establish a research contract. They do not establish that the original engine restores a particular dose correctly. The gap is a tested mapping from actual exposure and interruption reason to a decision, with independent follow-up.

## Evidence and contradictions
Targeted search of randomized studies, cohort analyses, reviews and consensus guidance on November 4. Sought null progression effects, conflicting workload metrics, post-race rest comparators, retained fitness and limits to injury-return protocols. Narrative review, largely abstract-level. Absence of a direct trial below means none identified in this pass.

| ID | Population, finding and limit |
|---|---|
| S1 | [Buist](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17940147/): 532 novice runners assigned to 13-week graded or 8-week standard programs. Injury incidence was 20.8% versus 20.3%. A 10%-based program did not demonstrate prevention. Whole programs differed, leaving the isolated effect of any single increment unresolved. |
| S2 | [Frandsen](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2024-109380): 5,205 adult Garmin runners, 588,071 sessions. Relative to regression or increases up to 10% over the prior 30-day longest run, adjusted overuse-injury hazard ratios were 1.64, 1.52 and 2.28 across successively larger spike categories. Weekly change was null and the ACWR pattern did not support the usual positive threshold. Exposure categories were nonmonotonic, the endpoint self-reported, and no prevention policy was assigned. |
| S3 | [RUNSAFE baseline study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39713859/): among 7,391 runners, established frequent runners had fewer reported injuries per accumulated distance in some comparisons. Trained survivors differ from athletes newly adding a day. Related cohort to S2. [Fredette systematic review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34478518/) found inconsistent relations across training parameters. [Impellizzeri](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32502973/) details methodological problems with a single acute:chronic ratio. |
| S4 | [Run Clever randomized trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29527322/): no significant overall injury difference between recreational runners assigned intensity-focused versus volume-focused progression. Uncertainty and attrition limit inference. It does not compare threshold-first with speed-first re-entry or establish equivalence. |
| S5 | [Chen](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33517866/) observed changes after two weeks of cessation in male endurance athletes. [Detraining review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38344385/) distinguishes short and longer cessation, partial training and variable physiological responses. Loss of aerobic performance and loss of tolerance for running impact are different outcomes. Neither paper supplies an individual return-dose curve. |
| S6 | [Mujika taper review](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0838.2010.01189.x): trained competitors may preserve intensity as volume declines before competition. This challenges blanket intensity removal in healthy athletes. A pre-race performance endpoint tells us little about routine deload timing, symptomatic athletes or return after an illness. |
| S7 | [Martínez-Navarro randomized recovery study](https://doi.org/10.1080/17461391.2020.1857441): 64 marathon finishers, 54 men. Moderate continuous running from 48 hours did not worsen measured muscle-damage markers versus rest through eight days. Jump performance favored running at one time point. No interval-readiness, injury-prevention or universal 48-hour clearance endpoint. |
| S8 | [IOC respiratory infection consensus](https://www.casem-acmse.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/1066.full_.pdf): severity and abnormal responses during, immediately after and 24 hours after a graded challenge inform medical return decisions. Consensus guidance for acute respiratory infection. Transfer to all illnesses or a watch-based clearance is unsupported. |
| S9 | [George et al., tibial bone stress injury scoping review](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-024-02051-y): clinical criteria include pain-free walking, local findings, functional capacity and radiological healing where indicated for high-risk injuries. Distance generally precedes speed. Underlying recommendations have limited direct comparisons and depend on injury site. |
| V1 | [StrideLab frequency review](2026-11-04-frequency-progression-and-score-limitations.md), [session-spike addendum](2026-11-04-running-frequency-and-session-spike-evidence.md), [matched-frequency protocol](experiments/experiment-007-runna-matched-frequency.md). Runna describes slower builds for six/seven days and separate first-week-back and long-run safeguards. Batch 3 documents skip/reschedule/realign options. These are vendor descriptions. Matched outputs, proprietary cutoffs and effectiveness remain unverified. |

## Decision model
At each plan review compute one primary state, the allowed session types, a reason, the evidence still missing and the observation that will trigger review. Use completed sessions and later response. A calendar date alone cannot unlock a transition. Evaluate the following precedence from any state.

1. Clinical stop or unresolved restrictions (C8/C9) → DEFER_INTENSITY with **hold all running** where indicated. State name alone never implies easy running is permitted.
2. Confirm coverage and reason for absence (C6). Unknown → MAINTAIN with scheduling paused for the affected dose pending reconciliation. Confirmed loss of familiar exposure → REBUILD_EXPOSURE with quality and long-run permissions tracked separately.
3. An unexpectedly difficult session → REDUCE the next affected dose (C4). A pattern of difficulty or a substantial race → consider DELOAD across sessions (C5/C7). Reassess after the next response. Persistent or worsening symptoms return to clinical review.
4. During rebuilding, familiar easy running with adequate follow-up may unlock RESTORE_LONG_RUN (C10) **or** RESTORE_QUALITY (C11/C12), according to the demands already demonstrated. If quality is requested before its gate clears, emit DEFER_INTENSITY and retain any independently allowed easy exposure. When both restorations qualify, propose only one dose change, selected by goal and athlete preference. Reassess before the other. A failed restoration returns to REDUCE or clinical hold.
5. Outside recovery, if evidence and goals support one increment, PROGRESS (C1/C2/C4). Otherwise MAINTAIN. After any accepted change, repeat this evaluation with the next actual exposure. A rejected proposal leaves the approved plan intact.

## How to test the machine
Start with paired counterexamples. Hold weekly mileage constant and shift distance into one outing; the session review should change. Hide a week of imports versus confirm a week off; only the latter supports cessation. Compare healthy taper versus febrile absence at identical mileage; the second must reach the clinical gate. Compare an experienced marathoner returning from a long gap with a novice who completed the same current easy exposure: past experience can identify familiar sessions but cannot override a restriction or stale exposure. A novice with no fast-run history cannot “restore” intervals. An intermediate runner can have extensive easy mileage and no recent threshold history. Classify experience per session type, retain training age and baseline continuity as separate modifiers, and report unknown experience explicitly.

Run those fixtures first for invariant violations, then use E011/E012 under Batch 8F consent/provenance gates. Pre-register endpoint definitions and comparison margins before viewing outcomes. Separate immediate completion from next-day symptom/effort response, repeated tolerated completion, days to sustainable return, athlete overrides and alerts per athlete-week. Missing follow-up is unknown. Use blinded independent coaches for appropriateness, clinician adjudication for restricted cases, athlete-held-out forward-time evaluation with nonoverlapping history windows, athlete-level uncertainty intervals and strata by experience, break reason and partial versus complete cessation. Report sparse strata as inconclusive. Observational policy comparisons remain confounded by who was offered a workout. Do not equate an unchosen option with an observed outcome. Clinical boundary failures veto promotion even if aggregate metrics improve.
