# P2-B0 | Comparable easy running repeatability protocol, v0.1

**Status: protocol prepared, no observations collected or evaluated.** Track P2-B owns within-athlete repeatability. E010 is its narrower matched-HR protocol. Gate 1 baseline is `26a8331e642c888dd73acb94755dded063baab8d`. The independent re-review authorizes P2-F preparation. Active P2-B requires purpose-authorized, correctly linked repeated segments from P2-F. Confirmatory access additionally requires the independently signed P2-B Gate 2 manifest. A silent, descriptive measurement has no workout decision authority.

## What a repeat measures

Primary endpoint is within-athlete typical error (TE, m/s) in **measured speed during a 20-minute easy segment at comparable HR** on different days under the same protocol. No physiological threshold or individualized pace correction is estimated. The comparator is the same athlete's unadjusted usual easy speed on the same route, using the same ordered pair dates and QC, without requiring HR/exposure matching. Also report all comparator-eligible days to reveal the cost of matching. Independently tabulate the secondary fixed-speed stage; HR control and speed control answer different questions.

All numerical windows below are proposed operational settings for feasibility, including the earlier Session D 3 bpm, 3 °C and 10 percentage-point limits. Changes require a version, reason and new untouched confirmatory partition. Session D provides evidence and contrary findings for these choices. Its retest evidence is protocol-specific. HR drift can repeat without agreeing with individual criterion changes in threshold speed.

## Register opportunities before the run

Enroll only authorized adult outdoor easy runs scheduled as an easy effort; record all offered opportunities including declines, missing uploads and broken links. Create a protocol card per athlete and stage before the first attempt with route version and direction, surface, start marker, measured loop length/landmarks, ordered grade profile, elevation reference, warm-up route, intended start phase (morning/afternoon/evening and local clock time), instrument, control mode and target. Exclude intervals, races, trails with changing footing, treadmill and mixed-mode activities from this outdoor series. Any separate population needs its own version.

Use a familiar conversational effort. Agree a personal HR anchor from habitual easy running **before** observations, without age formulas. For the secondary speed-control series, record a preselected comfortable speed (m/s) and sequence it on separate opportunities. Each mode requires its own six eligible dates; incomplete secondary data never reduces the primary denominator. No athlete should chase a target to rescue a measurement; an interrupted effort remains recorded with its reason.

## Select pairs without reading endpoint differences

Assign six scheduled separate-day opportunities per mode across three to four weeks as three calendar pairs (1↔2, 3↔4, 5↔6), at the same point of the weekly cycle where feasible. Each pair must be 24 h–7 d apart; leave a disqualified pair unmatched. Record all extra runs outside the schedule; no replacement, reuse, favorable ordering or expansion after inspecting measurements. Retain actual spacing, local date and weekly training context. One owner is a feasibility case. A later separately authorized pilot may target 8–12 adults as proposed in G; six eligible days/three independent pairs per athlete enable a preliminary personal estimate only. No claim of power follows from this count.

Require within-pair whole-window HR difference ≤3 bpm in HR-control mode; in speed control require mean speeds within 3% of each other (absolute difference divided by pair mean). Require identical protocol, account and supported measurement method, environment and context rules above. Pair matching can inspect control variable and QC, never the paired endpoint difference. Separate common support by athlete, device, route and climate; no extrapolation across disconnected strata. Persist actual paired HR, temperature, RH, effort and workload differences. Count preassigned pair slots, eligible unique days, complete pairs and unrepresented strata.

## Descriptive quantities and error

* Primary speed is course distance/time in m/s with contemporaneous time-weighted mean HR in bpm. Report anchor and residual HR mismatch. Secondary HR at comparable speed is the time-weighted mean HR on the independent speed-control stage, with actual speed alongside it.
* For signed drift on the speed-control stage, split into first and last 600 s. Report late mean HR minus early mean HR (bpm), the corresponding speed difference and RPE; require comparable halves (speed difference / mean ≤3%, same surface, ordered grade and wind/sun exposure class). Unequal grade exposure or inadequate half-course evidence suppresses the secondary metric.
* For decoupling let each half's ratio be mean speed / mean HR. Report 100 × (1 − late ratio / early ratio), plus both raw HR and speed components. Positive values describe lower speed per heartbeat late. Apply the same half comparability and QC checks. Never convert a percentage to a universal pass/fail threshold.
* For each athlete and mode use chronological differences d = day 2 endpoint − day 1 endpoint for disjoint pairs. TE = sample SD(d)/√2 in the endpoint's units. Report pair count, signed mean difference and Bland–Altman limits (mean ±1.96 SD) descriptively. Show bias separately since constant drift can give TE zero. For independent approximately normal differences give a 95% interval for TE using chi-square limits with k−1 df. Three pairs are imprecise. If serial dependence or strong heteroscedasticity undermines those assumptions, label the interval unsupported; use a prespecified sensitivity on log speed, and block resampling across independent weeks/athletes only with enough blocks. Never treat samples/halves as independent runs.
* Later comparator report: estimate TE on identical date pairs for raw whole easy-run speed (post-warm-up timer distance/time, pauses excluded) and for registered segments. Compare bias and coverage as well as TE. On later held-out repeats use development-estimated bias ±1.96√2 TE as a prediction interval for paired differences; report empirical coverage, width and counts by athlete, climate and device. No between-athlete ICC substitutes for personal absolute error.

## Audit, falsifiers and release

Report a flow per account and subgroup: offered → consented → linked → sampled → QC-passing → segment eligible → matched → estimable. Give numerator/denominator and interval for each conditional step and overall; include zero-eligible athletes, withdrawals (aggregate only if permitted), burden minutes/prompts and declines. Code all reasons and one primary reason in order: rights/withdrawal, identity/link, wrong mode/route, time/pause, instrument QC, missing context, unstable effort, exposure mismatch, insufficient repeats. Record unknown outcomes separately. Identifiable coordinates or private activity keys must stay outside Git.

Predeclare sham baseline–baseline splits; mask HR, reverse route direction, swap sensor identity, insert pause, or erase weather/RPE in copies of permitted development records. Each required missing/mismatched input must force withdrawal of the affected metric from primary analysis. If any invalid pair passes, halt that extraction path. Compare the registered analysis against fixed 15-minute and 20-minute windows starting at the same marker, tighter HR ±2 bpm and temperature ±2 °C pair calipers, removal of the fastest eligible day (report pairs lost), unadjusted speed, GPS/optical-only strata, and independent results omitting high fatigue or post-strength days. For moisture sensitivity use actual vapor pressure in kPa = (RH/100) × 0.6108 × exp[17.27 T/(T+237.3)] with T in °C, as an alternate descriptor; report exposure distribution without adding duplicate humidity predictors. Sparse or reversing strata stay indeterminate.

A changed device/firmware, distance method, route version or direction starts a separate series. For a proposed device bridge collect at least three **paired simultaneous** recordings on separate eligible days, using the old and new devices on the same registered segment. Independently inspect signal loss, paired bias and 95% agreement interval against reviewer-frozen tolerances for both HR and speed. For a route change require a registered crossover on ≥3 pairs of separate, matched days, alternating old/new order; report route difference plus ordinary day-to-day uncertainty. A small bridge only establishes feasibility. Pool only if interval precision, direction bias and common support meet the frozen criterion; absent paired observations, out-of-margin interval or inconclusive bridge starts a new baseline and suppresses cross-series change.

Before any confirmatory access, freeze δ (worthwhile change in m/s), acceptable bias, minimum empirical interval coverage and width, minimum eligible fraction per target stratum, burden cap and bridge tolerances using development-only data and blinded athlete/reviewer input, with named signers, partition and version. Keep pilot and future held-out athletes/dates disjoint as applicable; no parameter selection from held-out repeats. Compare uncertainty in change (single pair: 1.96√2 TE; block means of independent days: 1.96 TE√(1/n₁+1/n₂)) to δ, using the upper uncertainty bound for TE. If unsupported precision, excessive bias, loss of common support, missing rights/linkage, failed controls, high exclusion/burden or unstable bridge prevents a preregistered requirement, return **not usable for the proposed purpose/stratum**; insufficient observations return **insufficient evidence**. No active release with unset margins. A repeated measurement alone cannot authorize progression, intervention or fitness claims.
