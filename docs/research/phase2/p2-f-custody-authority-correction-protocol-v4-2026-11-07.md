## Minimal authority primitive

For namespace N, an authenticated trusted authority endpoint must implement a linearizable conditional `Commit(expected_epoch, expected_generation, operation_id, request_digest, resulting_authority_root, resulting_inventory_root, disposition)`. It must compare the *full* expected state and any relevant current credential/revocation/withdrawal state, and atomically create an immutable N-scoped operation entry with request digest and final disposition plus the new state. A single expected state yields at most one state-advancing winner. Identical ID/digest returns its original final result; ID reused with another digest is rejected and triggers investigation. A stale generation or epoch cannot overwrite current state. Rejected requests that are to be replayable must themselves be durably registered under explicit authorization and ordering; authorization failures need not disclose registry contents. A full result must be reconstructible, not just a digest. The state and registry cannot be rolled back independently.

Strictly required: non-equivocating currentness across restore/replacement; atomic operation-ID binding; ordered withdrawals and the *actual* release boundary; independent identity and authorization; complete withdrawal-source coverage; and fail-closed ambiguity. Local files, SQLite, locks and signed historical receipts are cache/evidence only; none prove an ID was unconsumed or an old generation current. A monotonic number alone does not bind the history. Operation disposition is computed by the trusted service, not accepted blindly from a client.

## Coordinator candidate and hard boundary

Candidate: a dedicated authority service backed by an independently administered etcd 3.6 quorum, with a separately governed continuity witness and a sole-path output gateway. [etcd API](https://etcd.io/docs/v3.6/learning/api/) documents atomic multi-key comparisons/transactions, revisions, cluster IDs and default linearizable reads; [guarantees](https://etcd.io/docs/v3.6/learning/api_guarantees/) describe consensus and uncertainty after timeouts. [RBAC](https://etcd.io/docs/v3.6/op-guide/authentication/rbac/) and [transport security](https://etcd.io/docs/v3.6/op-guide/security/) are mechanisms, not protection from a root administrator. Watches are not a substitute for a linearizable current read.

Proposed trust root: independently pinned coordinator and witness identities and a separately governed enrollment record. Broker, withdrawal submitter, gateway, recovery officer and service administrator have distinct individually revocable mTLS identities and scoped roles. The trusted coordinator enforces operation type, subject, namespace, expected state, withdrawal scope and revocation; clients cannot write arbitrary etcd keys. etcd prefix permissions are defense in depth, not semantic transaction authorization. Distinct cluster members communicate over mutually authenticated TLS and use a quorum; compromised quorum/privileged cluster operator is outside integrity protection unless an independently enforced witness prevents activation. A database administrator cannot be assumed constrained by database RBAC.

The intended transaction compares epoch, generation, current roots, credential policy, withdrawal state and ID absence, then stores new roots/state and ID/digest/disposition in one transaction. Generation is a namespace counter, not a Raft term. COMMITTED_REVISION is etcd's KV modification revision bound to the transaction; it is not itself a continuity proof. Changes to policy/revocation must participate in the same serialization, not rely on an old TLS connection or cached credential. A stale caller is rejected and must re-read; quorum/partition loss denies progress. Separate per-namespace grants and opaque random namespace identifiers limit accidental cross-access, but metadata and timing remain linkable.

## Continuity and restore

Definitions for a future design: COORDINATOR_EPOCH is a unique 128-bit epoch bound by an independently protected enrollment/witness record to coordinator identity, namespace and genesis/previous epoch. COMMITTED_REVISION is the exact coordinator revision and namespace generation/root/registry commitment of an acknowledged transaction. CONTINUITY_PROOF would include authenticated current witness response to a fresh challenge, witness identity and epoch, last accepted generation/revision, authority/inventory/registry commitments and a verified chain from a separately trusted genesis, including a binding to the coordinator response. Broker and gateway must independently verify identity, live challenge response, epoch, exact commitment and chain, and require an atomic protocol that prevents withdrawal or another commit racing after verification.

A mere periodically witnessed checkpoint fails: an acknowledged operation after the last checkpoint disappears on restore. A witness must participate durably before *every* acknowledged authority/output/withdrawal decision, maintain its own independent nonrollback state, and forbid forks or expose conflict before any decision is used. A delayed checkpoint or old signature cannot prove currentness. A separate witness with a linearizable CAS registry essentially becomes part of the authority root; that witness's own restore, admin compromise, quorum and bootstrap need explicit independent trust and recovery. A fresh response alone cannot establish that the witness itself was not rolled back. [RFC 9162](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9162.html) gives inclusion and consistency proof constructions but warns that consistent views across parties require additional mechanisms; transparency alone does not establish currentness or prevent split views.

Backup/restore: stop all decisions and gateway release, revoke or fence old coordinator and credentials independently, compare the entire recovered history and operation registry with independently durable witness state, reject missing acknowledged decisions or divergent roots; no backup is authority. A revision bump cannot reconstruct missing writes; [etcd recovery](https://etcd.io/docs/v3.6/op-guide/recovery/) is not a nonrollback security protocol. If full exact state and global fencing cannot be proven, remain CLOSED, including permanent loss of availability. Replacement requires independently approved new epoch linking complete previous commitment, revoked old identity and proof no old cluster can serve authority. No actual witness, independent nonrollback enrollment or cross-service atomic protocol has been justified. Therefore no selection is made.

## Withdrawal and actual publication

Scope must include all original and derived outputs, exports, local files, backups, logs and network destinations; any bypass makes the invariant false. Source withdrawal is authenticated and becomes SOURCE_WITHDRAWAL_ACCEPTED only at the authoritative serialization point, not at source request receipt or broker delivery. Source requesters must not be told it was accepted before this point; waiting may be unbounded. Completeness requires identifying every source and an authoritative live channel covering all sources; that coverage is not yet specified.

OUTPUT_BEGIN registers ID, exact scope/destination/content digest and expected state; it is neither release nor a lease. Desired OUTPUT_COMMIT is the **first irreversible availability of any authority-sensitive byte to a recipient or destination outside the controlled gateway boundary**, not a database reservation, send return, queue insertion or recipient observation. OUTPUT_REJECTED_AFTER_WITHDRAWAL is a durable coordinator disposition after acceptance; bytes must not be released for it. The required relation is that an actual OUTPUT_COMMIT before acceptance is recorded as escaped; after acceptance no OUTPUT_COMMIT can occur. Already escaped information cannot be recalled.

A sole gateway could fence new attempts while acceptance is pending, prevent other egress, and hold acceptance until every in-flight operation is either known to have escaped or conclusively canceled and its durable escape record is anchored. Yet a gateway can crash after a byte escapes and before durable recording, or a byte can remain buffered in a kernel, network, proxy or receiver and become visible after a decision. Socket completion does not establish recipient observation; the [POSIX send specification](https://pubs.opengroup.org/onlinepubs/9799919799/functions/send.html) explicitly does not guarantee delivery. Cross-system atomicity of a database commit and arbitrary external visibility is not established. Recording “escaped” before sending is conservative accounting but does not prevent delayed actual visibility after acceptance. No timeout or cached lease proves a channel drained.

Files, backup and derived artifacts have the same problem: rename, remote copies and reader access must be included in the actual boundary; ordinary filesystem writes do not automatically share coordinator atomicity. Batches need independently controlled release of every byte or a demonstrably atomic recipient-side transaction. Recipient unavailability must not generate a deferred release after acceptance. Gateway or broker crash, coordinator crash, network partition, lost responses, unknown partial write, uncertain duplicate or destination outage freeze acceptance and output until an independently verified drain/fence or adjudication; in some cases this can permanently block acceptance. Retrying with same ID queries the authoritative registry; unknown release state never triggers blind retransmission. A trustworthy recipient cooperating in the *same* commit boundary, or proven complete fencing/draining, may narrow the problem but is not established here. Gateway admin can bypass controls if malicious; isolation, recovery and channel coverage are not proven. Consequently OUTPUT_COMMIT is a desired externally visible event, not a claimed implementable atomic database event.

## Retry and concurrency

A first valid request uses the coordinator transaction and immutable operation registry. An identical retry returns exactly its prior final disposition and committed roots; a different digest for that ID fails closed. A lost response or crash after commit triggers a linearizable registry query with continuity verification; a crash before commit is retried only after authoritative proof of absence and current state. A restored local DB or broker reinstall is a cache miss, never evidence of non-consumption. If the authoritative service is unavailable or restored continuity is unproven, no answer or retry may establish currentness. Concurrent requests compare the same generation; only one wins, the other gets a deterministic conflict and may be freshly resubmitted with a new ID after policy re-evaluation, never silently rebased. A restrictive request immediately fences the submitting gateway locally but is not accepted until authoritative commit; any permissive operation ordered after acceptance is rejected. This desired behavior is conditional on solving continuity and output serialization.

## Canonical representation — proposed profile, incomplete

Use [RFC 8949 section 4.2.1](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8949.html#section-4.2.1) core deterministic CBOR, not an ad-hoc serializer. Proposed request is a definite-length map with unsigned integer keys and no tags, floats, indefinite values, nonminimal integers, duplicate keys, unknown fields or trailing bytes. All fields are mandatory: 0 schema version unsigned integer = 1; 1 namespace ID bstr 16; 2 operation ID bstr 16; 3 requester identity reference bstr 32 (digest of separately specified credential identity); 4 request type uint 0..255; 5 expected epoch bstr 16; 6 expected generation uint 0..2^63-1; 7 expected coordinator revision uint 0..2^63-1; 8 previous authority root bstr 32; 9 resulting authority root bstr 32; 10 inventory root bstr 32; 11 withdrawal state uint 0..2; 12 requested disposition uint 0..255; 13 continuity reference bstr 32. The coordinator computes the actual disposition. Request digest is SHA-256 of ASCII `StrideLab/P2F/request/v1`, one zero byte, then the exact deterministic request bytes; the digest is stored outside those bytes to avoid circularity. Reject all noncanonical inputs by parsing with duplicate-key detection, validating types/ranges, deterministic re-encoding and byte equality. Do not normalize alternate logical identities implicitly.

Authority/inventory root state schemas, exact enums, credential identity binding, continuity-reference construction and migration for every historic state have not been frozen, so the format as a whole is INCOMPLETE. No unrecognized version is interpreted as v1; a future migration must be an explicitly authorized, witnessed transition binding both old and new roots without losing old operation-ID outcomes. No hash claims nonrollback or secrecy.

## Local Windows durability and offline

Local state is an untrusted recovery cache. Missing, corrupt, stale, power-loss-ambiguous or restored state is discarded or quarantined and reconciled against the current independently verified coordinator and full authoritative inventory/registry; if unavailable, deny. Local rollback cannot advance the coordinator *provided the proposed remote continuity assumptions hold*. No NTFS rename/flush or device power-loss guarantee is asserted. Windows crash/persistence testing is still target evidence, but local atomic durability no longer needs to be the antirollback authority primitive. This scope reduction does not make the overall design ready.

Authority-sensitive work requires coordinator, witness and controlled output channel online. `OFFLINE_AUTHORITY_SENSITIVE_OPERATION_NOT_SUPPORTED`. Ordinary non-sensitive local app functions may continue separately only when they cannot read, derive, export or expose protected material and have an independently enforced separation.

## Threat model freeze

| Actor | Boundary and consequence |
|---|---|
| Ordinary interactive user | In scope for intended authorization checks; enforcement remains unvalidated. |
| Same-token malicious process | Out of scope for executable isolation; may impersonate or use reachable credentials and access outputs available to that token. |
| Stolen broker credential | Out of scope for identity theft; attacker can act within granted scope until ordered revocation; identity is not executable integrity. |
| Workstation admin, SYSTEM, kernel | Out of scope; can alter broker, bypass local controls or access data/keys; close and investigate. |
| Remote coordinator admin | Out of scope if able to rewrite quorum/history; independent witness is intended to detect, not proven to prevent all actions. |
| Coordinator cluster operator/member | Compromised quorum/operator can rewrite/deny; single-member fault tolerance is not Byzantine security. |
| Continuity-witness administrator | Out of scope if able to rewrite/reset witness; can destroy the anchor or equivocate unless independently governed controls hold. |
| Output-gateway administrator | Out of scope if able to read or emit data; can bypass the release fence. |
| Network attacker | In scope for authenticated transport and integrity assuming valid keys; denial/partition closes. |
| Restored local backup | In scope as untrusted cache, never proof of currentness; confidentiality of copied data remains separate. |
| Restored coordinator backup | Detection is required but not proven, so all authority closes. |
| Compromised client executable | Out of scope for endpoint integrity; may disclose data and misuse authorized credentials; stop and revoke. |

Credential revocation cannot recall previously escaped material; compromises spanning independent trust domains defeat separation. Operational identity, enrollment, cluster/member governance and secure credential issuance/revocation remain unimplemented.
