## 1. Explicit findings and decisions

1. The plaintext F2.2 SQLite prototype, `SyntheticAuthority`, current F2.1 in-memory authority, and private command are not an authenticated, nonrollback custody boundary. The private command checks a steward-supplied `current.json` and an operational lock; its documented operator prerequisites are not proof that a separately verified live authority, protected keys, or deletion service exists. Treat it as a bounded probe, not as a security component admitted for this new design.
2. A signature or digest on an old grant proves origin or integrity, not freshness. A restored data store, old signed authority snapshot, duplicated local disk, or local wall clock cannot establish current revocation state. An independent currentness anchor and a fail-closed restore barrier are required. Encryption alone does not prevent an authorized stale reader from opening withdrawn data.
3. For this proposal, choose **one current authority service in a separate trust and restore domain**. Its operator and technology must be selected and reviewed before implementation is cleared for real intake. It may be a dedicated local steward-controlled service rather than a cloud service, but it must provide the linearizable and nonrollback properties below; a different directory, append-only file or second copy alone does not prove those properties. If the service cannot be contacted, currentness is unknown: no ordinary processing, even offline. No cached-grant grace period.
4. The first candidate is one adult owner's **direct original Garmin device FIT Activity file**, no API or third-party conversion. Model, firmware, transfer steps, SDK/parser and independent reference are not yet selected or validated. The existing private operator command is TCX-only; it does not establish this FIT path. If direct FIT ownership, format, provenance, permission, terms or independent decoding cannot be established, stop; do not silently substitute TCX or a provider API.
5. Review found no authority, key custody, legal decision or independent device audit to approve. The previous independent synthetic pass remains limited to SQLite plus one shared in-process test authority. This document's threat exercise is self-review, not an independent security signoff.

## 2. Trust boundary and invariants

The smallest deployable pilot is one named participant/account, one source family, one original activity and one development-only audit on an approved isolated workstation, with no production coaching or confirmatory reuse. The participant and authorized steward have distinct roles even if the same person holds them; document role overlap and obtain independent security and reference review. Local custody and authority may be on the same isolated site only if they are genuinely independent recovery/administrative domains and the current authority can prove nonrollback under the selected threat model. Do not infer this property from physical separation alone.

**I1 Default deny:** unknown identity, grant, alias, source, purpose, partition, requested operation, lineage, device enrollment, authority generation or freshness means no plaintext exposure, query, projection, coaching state, UI or export. Deny as well on unavailable/corrupt anchor, ambiguous identity, invalid authentication, uncertain clock or failed integrity check.

**I2 Identity:** a separately authenticated participant authorizes their own account and specified source, while a steward verifies the account binding and receipt. A device serial, FIT metadata, byte hash, file name, activity timestamp or bearer of a file cannot by itself prove ownership. Bind source family and aliases by independent reviewed evidence, not file self-assertion or similarity. Scope every key and edge by account and purpose. Unknown or conflicting aliases quarantine.

**I3 Minimum authority:** permission equals the intersection of current participant choice, reviewed source/platform permissions, controller policy, granted operation, scope, partition and current status; no individual component broadens another. Grant origination requires participant authentication/verified receipt and steward authorization; withdrawal may be made by participant through the stated verified channel, or the steward may immediately restrict processing. Neither operator nor custodian may widen a grant. Authentication assurance and an accessible out-of-band withdrawal channel must be documented and tested for the named pilot.

**I4 Currentness:** every process begins closed after launch, crash, reinstall, migration or restore; it obtains a fresh authenticated current decision from the separately controlled authority, with account/source/purpose/view/partition/operation, current committed generation, suppression and permitted expiry. A signature verifies issuer and exact bytes; a separately protected latest-generation/current-state check verifies freshness. Never accept the data backup's own counter as proof of currentness. An old device that cannot obtain current authority remains closed. Treat unrelated accounts independently, without leaking their existence.

**I5 Exposure barrier:** authorize and resolve ownership *before any content decryption or key release*, using minimal authenticated metadata; then verify authenticated ciphertext, subject binding and lineage before interpreting plaintext. Repeat authorization at every protected operation and before exposing/publishing its result. An OS may decrypt an encrypted volume transparently: full-disk encryption alone is not an application authorization barrier. If the chosen platform cannot deny key use/content decryption on withdrawal, the path does not satisfy this contract. OS privileged administrators, already-open memory and participant-owned copies require explicit residual-risk and response analysis; do not claim they are technically recalled.

**I6 Concurrency:** all read/decrypt/output operations and grant/withdrawal transitions must share a verified cross-process coordination boundary with a defined linearization point. If read wins, it must finish or cancel and discard unpublished plaintext before withdrawal is acknowledged; if withdrawal wins, the read denies. Immediately stop accepting new work on a withdrawal request, close existing UI/export channels and make the request durably visible; acknowledge completed suppression only after in-flight work and key access are fenced. Never silently accept a stale lease or retry through contention. A time-limited token alone does not meet immediate revocation. Escaped copies cannot be recalled: minimize them and document containment and response.

**I7 Recoverability and evidence:** append-only restricted lineage and inventories identify originals, aliases, grants, descendants, keys, storage and processors; a protected audit records dispositions without embedding raw signals. Recovery never silently changes authority state. Destruction of data and retention of the minimum authorized suppression evidence are distinct decisions.

## 3. State, operations and durability

Persist authenticated, versioned authority records with opaque account and source-family references, grant ID, issuer/participant attestation reference, authority generation and previous revision, permitted operations and purpose, deciding view, development-only partition, source/path/alias restrictions, effective/expiry times and clock basis, receipt/terms versions, status and supersession/reason references. A participant-facing receipt is kept separately in restricted custody. Use explicit states `PENDING -> ACTIVE -> SUSPEND_REQUESTED -> SUPPRESSED -> DISPOSITION_PENDING -> CLOSED`; `EXPIRED` also denies. A deletion request can transition directly to suppression. A narrower replacement grant never silently reactivates a revoked broader grant. Any later reauthorization is separately authenticated, limited and reviewed; it cannot undo permanent pilot exclusion from confirmatory data.

The authoritative decision and monotonic generation must be committed durably before acknowledging grant/revoke. Stop access on partial, unverifiable or ambiguous transition. On crash during withdrawal, startup consults current independent authority and resumes suppression/disposition before any ordinary access; a failed durability write leaves the affected scope closed and triggers escalation. A low-risk audit timestamp is not a substitute for an authenticated current generation. Persist a minimal protected suppression/alias record only with a documented lawful basis, content, purpose, custody and expiry; if it cannot lawfully be retained, deny unresolved aliases and prevent confirmatory reuse without inventing a mapping.

Define a current-authority API/lease contract before coding: authenticated principal and service binding; fresh decision or deny; mutually exclusive operation and revoke fencing with monotonic generation; lease acquisition, expiry, fencing and release; atomic acknowledgement and crash recovery; key-release authorization; and audit reference. Pin identity of the authority and verify issuer rotation. Expiry requires a trustworthy clock and must fail closed on rollback, drift or unknown time. An attacker able to roll back every trusted anchor, impersonate the authority or control privileged operator accounts is outside ordinary local-storage protections; detect where possible and halt. A hash chain, Keystore key, hardware-backed key or signed old response alone is not a monotonic authority.

## 4. Storage, keys and deletion

Use established authenticated encryption with a vetted platform implementation and unique nonces; no bespoke cryptography. Bind ciphertext to account, object identity, lineage and scope as authenticated metadata. Key generation uses the platform CSPRNG. Separate per-account data-encryption keys and key-encryption/authority credentials; partition keys prevent one account's decryption from granting another's. Keep recovery keys under a distinct named custodian and outside data backups, with least privilege, access logging, tested rotation and documented loss/recovery policy. A revocation must fence key release and all processing paths; destruction of a key supports deletion only when every usable key copy and plaintext descendant is accounted for. Do not claim guaranteed hardware-backed storage or secure physical erasure without device-specific evidence.

Android Keystore is a possible future key-protection mechanism, not a rollback-resistant authorization service and not an implementation choice for the current Windows/offline research command. [Android Keystore](https://developer.android.com/privacy-and-security/keystore) describes platform key protection; [Android backup](https://developer.android.com/identity/data/autobackup) notes that device-to-device behavior can differ even with `allowBackup=false`. Configure and test backup and transfer exclusions on the actual version/device; do not rely on a manifest flag alone. Store no credentials, private hashes, IDs, raw samples, exact dates, source paths, coordinates or rare configurations in Git, CI, logs, crash reports, AI services or normal analytics. Test fixture bytes are invented and contain no human records.

Inventory and isolate original, imported working copies, normalized events, aliases, links, traces, segments, computed exposures, projections, athlete-state contributions, summaries, temporary files, viewers, thumbnails, caches, swap/recovery, logs, exports, derivatives, keys and all backups. Tag each object with account, source family, purpose, partition, parents, version, retention and accountable custodian in a restricted inventory. Avoid creating model/research artifacts from this pilot; if any were later specifically approved, invalidate/dispose or retrain inseparable artifacts under a separately approved procedure, not merely delete source rows. On withdrawal: stop access and suppress; enumerate descendants; delete or isolate every controlled copy; rebuild permitted aggregates from remaining eligible inputs; invalidate stale materializations and splits; verify independently. Any unknown descendant or unverified copy blocks completion and further intake. Irreversible lawfully released information cannot be recalled: explain this before consent and record its residual exposure. Never equate pseudonymization, suppression, logical deletion, key destruction or backup expiry with verified physical erasure.

Keep only minimum restricted audit records under an approved retention basis: actor role/pseudonymous reference, action and coarse time where sufficient, authorization and authority generation, source/receipt and version references, allow/deny reason category, restore/deletion disposition and verifier. Raw identity mapping, full file digest and exact timestamps are more restricted than general audit; never assume audit is anonymous. Prevent event existence and account enumeration across scopes. Participant requests, processor obligations, legal holds and disclosure require a jurisdiction- and study-specific determination by the responsible controller; this proposal does not state a universal legal rule.

## 5. Backup, restore and re-enrollment

Default for the first pilot: no ordinary cloud or automatic device backup; any necessary encrypted recovery copy must be explicitly approved, inventoried, protected separately, time-limited and tested. Restoration occurs in access-closed scratch with network sync, indexing, processing, UI and exports disabled. Authenticate current authority from the independent domain *before* allowing key release or ordinary content read. Keep restored authority data inert; it must never replace the live authority.

| Case | Required behavior |
|---|---|
| A. Pre-withdrawal backup restored after withdrawal | Current authority denies; do not decrypt for ordinary reads. Suppress aliases, purge/block descendants and restored copies; verify B remains isolated. A permitted privileged recovery procedure cannot expose content to normal roles. |
| B. Pre-deletion backup restored after deletion | The current deletion/suppression record wins. Apply deletion and invalidate all descendants before read; if retained backup cannot be deleted, isolate through documented expiry and separately verify rotation. |
| C. Old device returns | Treat it as untrusted/stale. No offline access. Reauthenticate device and participant, obtain current generation, then reconcile only within current scope; reject old key/lease and quarantined aliases. Do not infer reauthorization. |
| D. Data restored, current authority absent/corrupt/unreachable | Closed indefinitely until independently recovered and freshness established; never bootstrap authority from the data backup or a locally stored signed grant. |
| E. Authority restored, data missing or older | Establish authority freshness against the independent anchor; if that cannot be established, remain closed. Verify every data version, ownership, integrity and deletion marker against current authority/inventory; quarantine missing/older data and never manufacture history. |

For a lost, reset, rolled-back or reinstalled authority anchor: invalidate local grants, sessions and device enrollments; suspend intake and ordinary reads; require a separately authenticated recovery procedure with the independent reviewer, current withdrawal and exclusion inventory, key custodian and recorded fresh epoch. If current state cannot be proven, do not re-enable it. A participant's previous withdrawal remains effective. Migration requires new device enrollment and fresh current authority; a transfer cannot carry access merely by copying application data. Explicitly test clock rollback and signed-grant replay. Simultaneously losing all independent state cannot be repaired from stale data safely; stop rather than guess.

### Chosen candidate and rejected alternatives

Choose **B: one isolated Windows workstation and a broker-owned TPM 2.0 nonvolatile (NV) monotonic counter**, accessed by TPM Base Services (TBS), plus an authenticated authority ledger managed by one dedicated local Windows service account. The TPM NV index, its enrolled TPM identity/name and authorization policy are outside the ordinary application/data/authority-file backup and restore domain. The broker is the only process permitted to use the counter policy credential, authority ledger and encrypted objects. The TPM is the current-generation anchor, not a grant store: a protected ledger holds the grant and suppression state. Every operation must read the actual enrolled TPM NV index and authenticate the ledger state. One account is supported; no multi-provider service or hosted athlete data.

This is a **candidate design, not a demonstrated suitable anchor**. Windows TBS command policy, TPM ownership/provisioning rights, NV counter allocation, firmware behavior, index authorization, atomic increment/read and survival across reboot or interrupted power on the chosen OS/device are unverified. [Microsoft documents TBS command blocking](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/tbs/command-blocking); the [TPM architecture](https://trustedcomputinggroup.org/wp-content/uploads/TPM-Rev-2.0-Part-1-Architecture-01.07-2014-03-13.pdf) describes counter concepts, not a guarantee for this workstation. Do not clear/reset the TPM, change security policy, enumerate sensitive values or provision hardware as part of this review. If the exact required operations cannot be demonstrated safely and supported with independent review, reject this candidate; do not fall back to an ordinary local file or silently relax currentness.

| Alternative | Decision |
|---|---|
| Signed JSON, ordinary file, backup copy, or adjacent SQLite counter | Reject: integrity or location does not establish currentness and copies can roll back together. |
| A second local database or process-local lock alone | Reject: neither survives whole-machine rollback as independent proof of the newest state. |
| Android Keystore or Windows DPAPI alone | Reject as authority anchor: key protection does not by itself prevent stale grant replay or prove a monotonic current state. |

| TPM NV counter with separately protected local ledger and single broker | Selected candidate because it can anchor a generation outside ordinary file rollback; only conditional on actual device, policy, implementation, recovery and test evidence. |

### Currentness, issuance and crash ordering

The authority ledger is an authenticated, access-controlled, durable local database distinct from the data directory, carrying canonical records for account, source family, purposes, operations, partition, grant, status, previous generation and evidence reference. Its protection key belongs only to the broker's dedicated service identity. Account binding and receipt are established out of band; neither a local user nor file content self-asserts ownership. Store no authority or protected data on automatic cloud backup. A protected enrollment record binds the expected TPM public identity and NV index name/policy; it is itself checked against independently recorded enrollment evidence after reinstall or reset. Never let a restored ledger select or create a replacement anchor automatically.

For each operation the broker authenticates the local caller, reads current hardware counter `N`, validates enrolled anchor identity, reads and authenticates exactly one committed ledger head with generation `N`, and enforces scoped active state and suppression; mismatch, duplicate head, missing/corrupt state, reset identity, unknown policy or TPM error closes all data access. No cached authorization or stale signed record is accepted. Integrity means an authorized ledger writer authenticated the record; freshness means its one committed generation equals the freshly read enrolled counter; rollback resistance relies on the protected counter/index and restricted writer policy under the stated threat model, not equality of two backup files. The broker serializes authority transitions and permits at most one authenticated ledger state per counter generation. It must never sign a second state at an already-used generation. Protect issuance credentials and enforce exclusive issuance independently of attacker-writable files; this property is unproven until tested.

For a grant or restriction transition, the broker takes the exclusive gate, durably records a restricted transition intent and the proposed next state, atomically increments the TPM index, verifies the response **and** a fresh read, atomically commits and authenticates exactly one matching ledger state, flushes its durable journal, then acknowledges. A crash after increment with no matching committed state is locked closed; controlled recovery may only complete the already-authenticated restrictive intent, never invent an ACTIVE state. A missing intent, unverifiable counter or different head is closed. A request received but not durably anchored/acknowledged cannot be assumed to survive a crash; after **every** broker/OS restart, even one marked clean, the steward must positively reconcile the participant's authenticated withdrawal channel and pending requests before reopening. If that cannot establish whether a request was pending, remain closed. This explicit procedural fence is necessary because a crash before the first durable action leaves no technical proof of receipt. Do not acknowledge a withdrawal until it is anchored and all prior operations/copies are fenced.

### Revocation and key broker

One dedicated Windows broker process owns the only usable data-key access. Its service account uses a loaded, non-roaming local Windows profile, cannot be used for interactive login, and is protected by restrictive NTFS ACLs. Separately authenticated operator and steward accounts communicate through an ACL-restricted local named pipe; the broker validates the Windows client identity/role and the participant receipt. Pipe ACLs, client identity and service account boundaries must be verified on the selected installation. The service alone opens/decrypts/queries/produces the scoped result; it does not hand a raw data key or plaintext file handle to clients. No network listener, unapproved subprocess, command shell, uncontrolled debug or plugin access is permitted. OS administrators, a compromised broker, hardware replacement/clearing, arbitrary memory inspection and participant-owned copies are outside the ordinary-user isolation guarantee and must be explicitly risk-reviewed; they cannot be waved away by encryption.

The broker has a single exclusive operation gate covering authority read, TPM check, key unwrap, decrypt, query, projection and approved result delivery, as well as revoke. As soon as a verified withdrawal request is received, stop admission of new operations and queue the restrictive transition with priority; prevent further UI/export publication. If the read acquired the gate first, it must finish delivery before suppression acknowledgement, or be cancelled, its in-memory and temporary outputs discarded and keys released before acknowledgement. If revoke acquired it first, commit the restrictive state and deny that read. The **linearization point** is the successful fresh hardware/ledger comparison while holding the broker gate; for revoke, it is the hardware-counter and matching restrictive ledger commit while the gate remains held. The broker cannot withdraw plaintext already delivered: minimize output, inventory exports and disclose that boundary. Process termination, broker loss, lock/IPC error or ambiguous state closes all operations and forces startup reconciliation. All read processes must use the broker; ordinary users cannot access encrypted objects or DPAPI key material directly. A background independent writer or alternate decrypt path violates the design. This is a specification to test, not a claim the existing private command enforces it.

### Key custody, backups and operating assumptions

Select Windows CNG AES-256-GCM with CNG-generated random per-account data-encryption key; unique nonce per encryption under that key and authenticated account/object/purpose/version metadata. Generate and use through maintained platform crypto; do not implement cryptographic primitives. Wrap the account key with Windows user-scope `CryptProtectData` under the dedicated service account; **do not use machine-scope DPAPI**, which Microsoft states may permit any user on the machine to decrypt. [Microsoft DPAPI documentation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/dpapi/nf-dpapi-cryptprotectdata). Broker authorization is required before invoking `CryptUnprotectData`; DPAPI alone is not the fence and a privileged or compromised broker can bypass it. Use BitLocker for the local volume as an additional offline-device safeguard, not as application-layer authorization; its recovery key is held separately by the named key custodian. Original, temporary, normalized and output artifacts remain under broker-managed authenticated encryption. On withdrawal disable unwrap before acknowledgement, inventory/delete encrypted objects and descendants, and destroy the account key and all key copies where authorized; distinguish unverified physical erasure and irretrievable published/copied plaintext.

Use a current supported Windows workstation selected later, protected TPM 2.0, full-disk encryption, least-privilege dedicated service/operator accounts, verified local ACLs and an approved pinned offline parser. Disable cloud sync, telemetry, crash upload, indexing, unapproved backup, admin debugging, external AI and uncontrolled temporary files; keep no private paths, exact times, hashes or records in source control or routine logs. Default: **no data, keys, broker profile or authority backups**. If a backup is expressly approved, encrypt and inventory it separately, never back up the TPM anchor, and maintain controlled restore with fresh authorization; there is no promise a missing data key can be recovered. No private data or keys may be migrated to another workstation until an explicitly authorized, audited transfer is performed while the old current anchor and broker are available and a new anchor is enrolled. If the old anchor is lost, reset, replaced or cannot prove current status, do not decrypt/migrate old material and do not re-create an ACTIVE grant from backup. This may mean permanent data loss.

### Restore and bootstrap

**A pre-withdrawal backup:** current TPM/ledger says suppressed, so deny unwrap before reading source or descendants, inventory and dispose of the restored copy. **B pre-deletion backup:** current tombstone wins; deny and dispose, verify known descendants and backup expiry. **C old app/device state:** it is untrusted and cannot use the broker's keys or bypass a fresh current check; no cached grace, re-enrollment required. **D data without authority:** deny; never reconstruct authority from copied data or a signed stale grant. **E authority without data:** verify the enrolled TPM and exact committed state before any access; missing/old data is quarantined, not reconstructed or guessed. A data rollback with the same current authority must still verify each object version, deletion tombstone, grant and lineage against an independently current inventory; if this cannot detect a deleted/replaced object, restoration stays closed. Ordinary data generation stored only in a rolled-back data file is insufficient.

App reinstall requires trusted broker code/identity and TPM enrollment verification, then steward reconciliation of participant requests and live counter/ledger match. Restore into a closed scratch environment; no parser, viewer, auto-indexer or key unwrap until authority plus object inventory pass. Workstation migration needs a currently authorized, fenced handoff from the *live old* TPM/broker to the newly enrolled anchor, with a signed restricted manifest, independent verification and old-device access disabled; where proof, clock, key or approval is missing, abandon migration and keep the old data inaccessible. A TPM reset/clear or authority-database loss is **not** recovered by recreating the counter at a convenient value. Independently review any recovery proposal; otherwise closed. A crash during revoke forces restart reconciliation described above before any reads. For an uncertain half-committed transition, process it as suppressed until reviewed, never as active.

### Time and first source

Counter generations, not a clock, determine current authority ordering and rollback detection. However, the existing admission contract requires real-world grant and retention deadlines; a clock is still required to enforce them. Select a separately controlled **Network Time Security (NTS, RFC 8915) time-only gateway**, not a time server built by the pilot. The isolated data workstation has no general internet; a separately administered gateway may contact a named NTS service such as `time.cloudflare.com`, and sends only a fresh broker challenge-bound time attestation through an authenticated local link. The gateway must contain no athlete files or source identifiers; disclose its service/IP metadata and obtain approval. The attestation must include a documented uncertainty bound and an independently verified TLS/certificate-time bootstrap. Enforce expiry conservatively using the uncertainty bound and monotonic elapsed time; on sleep/resume, excessive delay, failed authentication, unknown certificate time, drift, rollback or gateway failure, close and reacquire fresh time. A signed stale time alone is not fresh. No implementation may claim this time design works until its bootstrap, bounds and withheld-network behavior have passed review; this may conflict with the existing fully offline runbook and requires an explicit scope/processor decision, otherwise **stop**. [RFC 8915](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8915.html) describes authentication, not a guarantee of accurate time or local bootstrap; [Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/time-services/nts/) describes its NTS endpoint. No network is needed for authority freshness itself.

Select as candidate source one directly copied, original Garmin-device-generated FIT Activity file, not a Connect API, third-party conversion or TCX substitution. Independently authenticate the participant/account and confirm device ownership and approved source terms before transfer. Freeze model/family, firmware, sensor epoch, exact direct-copy method, device/source time and offset where meaningful, ingest time, content hash and original immutable bytes privately. Choose `fitdecode==0.11.0` on CPython 3.12.10 only as the **candidate** decoder already used by the synthetic F3 fixture; pin its complete package artifact hash, dependencies, parser code/configuration and FIT profile before qualification. Select Garmin FIT SDK Python decoder as the **candidate independent reference**, subject to exact package/version/hash, license, offline operation and independently verified decoding lineage; two wrappers around a common decoder are not independent. [Garmin lists its Python FIT SDK](https://developer.garmin.com/fit/get-the-sdk/). Confirm CRC, supported message types, session/lap/record ownership, timestamps, fields and unknowns with generated fixtures before any real source. `p2_fit_trace` is a limited synthetic probe and B0-ineligible, and the existing private command is TCX-only. The actual Garmin model/firmware, original acquisition, source permissions, parser support and reference are **not pinned or verified**; human intake stays blocked.
